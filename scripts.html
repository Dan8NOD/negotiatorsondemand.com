10 Free Negotiation Scripts

Battle-tested scripts using tactical empathy. From a Marine vet with 1000+ live practice sessions.

1. The Salary Negotiation Opening

When they ask "What are your salary expectations?"

"I appreciate you asking. Before I throw out a number, it seems like you're trying to make sure we're in the same ballpark—which makes total sense. Can I ask what range you've budgeted for this role? That way I can be straight with you about whether we're aligned."

Tactical empathy: Labeling their intent ("seems like you're trying to...") defuses pressure and invites collaboration instead of combat.

2. The Deadline Push-Back

When they say "We need an answer by Friday."

"I hear you—sounds like Friday is critical on your end. Help me understand: what happens if we push to Monday? I want to give this the attention it deserves, and rushing feels like it could hurt both of us."

Tactical empathy: Mirroring their urgency ("sounds like Friday is critical") then asking "what happens if..." reveals whether the deadline is real or manufactured.

3. The Lowball Counter

When their offer is insultingly low.

"I appreciate the offer. It sounds like you're working within tight constraints. How am I supposed to accept this when it's 30% below market rate for someone with my experience?"

Tactical empathy: Calibrated question ("How am I supposed to...") forces them to solve your problem without sounding adversarial.

4. The Scope Creep Defense

When they ask for extras not in the original agreement.

"I want to help. It seems like this project has grown beyond what we originally scoped. If we add [new request], what should we remove to keep this on budget and on time?"

Tactical empathy: Label the expansion ("seems like this has grown") then use forced choice to make them prioritize.

5. The Discount Request Response

When they ask "Can you do better on price?"

"I get it—everyone wants the best deal. It sounds like price is your biggest concern right now. What would we need to take out to hit your number? I'd hate for you to lose features that matter."

Tactical empathy: Acknowledge their position ("sounds like price is your biggest concern") then reframe the negotiation from discount to scope.

6. The Non-Monetary Value Add

When salary is maxed but you want more.

"I understand the salary is locked. That's fair. It seems like you want to make this work as much as I do. What if we looked at remote flexibility, extra PTO, or a signing bonus? What's possible there?"

Tactical empathy: Label mutual interest ("seems like you want to make this work") then open new negotiation dimensions.

7. The Anchor Reset

When they anchor low before you can.

"I appreciate you sharing that range. It sounds like that's based on [junior role/different scope]. Given [your specific value], I was thinking more in the range of [higher number]. How does that land with you?"

Tactical empathy: Label their anchor's basis ("sounds like that's based on...") then reframe with your counter-anchor as a question.

8. The "Final Offer" Test

When they claim "This is our final offer."

"I respect that. It seems like you've gone as far as you can on your end. Before I respond—is there anyone else who'd need to sign off if we found creative solutions, or is this truly the ceiling?"

Tactical empathy: Label their constraint ("seems like you've gone as far as you can") while testing if higher authority exists.

9. The Comparison Deflection

When they say "But others charge less."

"You're right—there are cheaper options out there. It sounds like you're weighing cost against quality. What happens if you go with the lower price and it doesn't deliver? What's that risk worth?"

Tactical empathy: Agree with their fact ("you're right") then label their real fear and make them calculate risk.

10. The Graceful Exit

When you need to walk away without burning bridges.

"I've really appreciated this conversation. It seems like we're not quite aligned on [terms/price/scope] right now, and I don't want to waste your time. If anything changes on your end, I'd love to revisit this. Fair enough?"

Tactical empathy: Label the misalignment without blame, leave the door open, and confirm with "Fair enough?" to keep goodwill intact.
